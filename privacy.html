Privacy Policy

Money Laundering, a budgeting app by 615 Media Group, LLC · Effective November 9, 2026 · Updated November 13, 2026
The short version
  • Your transactions, balances and budgets live on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. We do not keep a copy.
  • One exception, and only if you turn it on: with Public shaming, the charges you mark as dirty are sent to our server so the friends you added can see them.
  • We never see your bank username or password. Bank linking is handled by Plaid.
  • We do not sell your data, show ads, or track you across other apps and websites.
  • If you subscribe so that we handle the bank connection, we hold the connection itself (a token) and nothing about what you spend.

Who we are

Money Laundering is a personal budgeting app for iPhone made by 615 Media Group, LLC ("we", "us"). Despite the name, it is a budgeting tool: it does not move, hold or launder money. Questions go to support@laundering.app.

Three ways to use the app

By hand (free). You type in your own transactions. No bank is connected, and what you type stays on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. We receive nothing about you or your money.

With your own Plaid keys (free). You create your own Plaid account and enter its keys in the app. The app then talks to Plaid directly from your iPhone. Your bank data never passes through us.

Connected by us (subscription, coming soon). We provide the Plaid connection. Your iPhone asks our server for your bank data, our server asks Plaid, and the answer is passed straight back to your iPhone.

What stays on your iPhone and in your iCloud

  • Transactions, balances, account names and the institutions you linked.
  • Your verdicts on charges, renamed merchants, recurring bills and income, savings goal and other settings.
  • If you use your own Plaid keys: those keys, in the iPhone Keychain (never in iCloud).

The app keeps a backup of your plan, your sorting, your goals, what you typed in by hand and your settings in your own iCloud (Apple's CloudKit), so they come back if you get a new iPhone. It is stored in your iCloud, under Apple's protections, and we cannot read it. You can turn the backup off in the app's Settings, and "Erase everything" deletes it. If you share a household with another person, the same data also syncs between your iCloud account and theirs. Face ID is handled by iOS; the app never receives your face or fingerprint data.

The town's clock

The games on Lint Lane ask our server what time it is, so changing your iPhone's clock cannot speed them up. The request carries nothing about you or your money. Like any internet request it shows your IP address, which stays only in the ordinary server log and is deleted after 30 days.

What we receive when we provide the connection

This section applies to the subscription service once it is available.

  • An account identifier from Sign in with Apple, and your email address only if you choose to share it.
  • Subscription status from Apple, so we know the subscription is active. We never see your payment card.
  • Bank connection tokens issued by Plaid, with the name of each institution, so we can fetch data on your behalf. They are stored encrypted.
  • Household membership, if you share with another person, so both phones reach the same connections.

Your transactions and balances pass through our server on their way from Plaid to your iPhone. We do not store them, read them, or use them for anything else. Server logs record that a request happened, when, and whether it failed. They do not contain transaction details and are deleted after 30 days.

Public shaming (optional, off until you turn it on)

Public shaming is a feed on the Rap Sheet tab where you and friends you have added see each other's poor choices and comment on them. Nothing below is collected unless you turn it on, accept its rules and pick a username. It needs Sign in with Apple, whichever way you use the app.

While it is on, we store on our server:

  • Your username and an account identifier from Sign in with Apple.
  • Your friends: who you invited or accepted, and anyone you blocked.
  • The charges you mark as dirty after turning it on: the shop's name, its category, the day, and the app's roast line. The amount is included only if you turn on "Show amounts to friends". You can take any single post back.
  • Comments and reactions you leave, and reports you file.

We never receive your account names, balances, income, bank details or anything about charges you marked clean or did not sort. Posts are shown only to you and to friends you have accepted, and are deleted after 30 days. Comments are checked by an automatic filter for abuse, and a reported comment or post is reviewed by a person. Turning Public shaming off in the app deletes your posts, comments, friends and username from our server at once. We do not use any of this for advertising and do not share it with anyone else.

Public profile (optional, off by default)

If you turn it on, we store a card that anyone with your link can see: your username, your washer, your Clean Streak steps, months in the green, the share of charges you called clean, fish caught, plants grown, and how Mouse and Mouse's House look. It never includes an amount, your income, a balance, your age or any charge. Turning it off deletes the card from our server at once.

Friends on Lint Lane (on once you have a friend; one switch turns it off)

Friends on Lint Lane uses your friends list from Public shaming. It turns on when you first have a friend there (you accepted an invite, or someone accepted yours): adding a friend is how you choose to play together. One switch turns all of it off again at any time (Lint Lane, Friends), and it then stays off until you turn it back on. Only friends you have accepted who have it on too can visit. While it is on we keep on our server: how Mouse's House looks (which things are out and where, how Mouse is dressed, your washer's style, your aquariums and the kinds of fish in them); which plant is in each bed of your garden, how far grown it is and how many different plants you have grown; the longest fish you have caught of each kind; and what friends leave at your house and you leave at theirs: a guestbook line or stamp picked from a fixed list (nobody can type a message), a reply picked from a fixed list, which plant was watered, and a small token, each with who left it and when. The look of the house follows your progress in the app, so a friend can tell roughly how far along you are; it holds no amounts. For this feature we never receive a transaction, an amount, a balance, your income, your daily number or your Suds.

A visitor to your house sees their own guestbook lines and those of people who are their friends too. Guestbook lines and gifts are deleted after 30 days (a gift sooner, when you put it away) and waterings after 9 days. Switching it off, leaving Public shaming or deleting your account deletes all of it at once, including what you left at friends' houses; removing or blocking a friend deletes what each of you left at the other's; and if the app on your phone has not been in touch for 60 days we close your house and delete it (it opens again when you next use the app, unless you have switched it off). You can report a guestbook line; we look at reports within 24 hours.

Fishing together

This is part of Friends on Lint Lane and follows the same switch. While it is on and you are on the pond or the pier in the app, friends you have accepted who have it on too can see that you are fishing, at which of the two spots, how many casts you have made since you arrived, and the last thing you caught (what it was and how long). Your phone sends this about every ten seconds while you are there. We keep one small record of it per person. Friends stop seeing it thirty seconds after your phone last checked in; we delete it when you leave the pond or the pier, and within a day if your phone could not tell us. It is also deleted when you switch Friends on Lint Lane off, leave Public shaming or delete your account. Nothing about your money, your Suds or your location, and nothing you type, is sent for this.

Plaid

When you link a bank you do so through Plaid, Inc. You give your bank credentials to Plaid or to your bank, never to us. Plaid collects and processes your information under its own End User Privacy Policy. You can see and revoke Plaid connections at my.plaid.com.

Apple

Purchases are processed by Apple. To draw the small map on a charge, the app sends that merchant's location to Apple Maps; it does not use or send your own location. If you agreed to share analytics with developers in iOS settings, Apple gives us anonymous crash and usage statistics.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent personal information.
  • We do not include advertising or analytics software from other companies in the app.
  • We do not use your financial information for advertising.

Keeping and deleting

You can delete your account in the app under Settings. When you do, we remove your bank connections at Plaid and delete your tokens and account record right away, and any remaining backups within 30 days. If your subscription ends, we remove your bank connections after 14 days. Deleting your account also deletes everything Public shaming holds about you. Deleting the app removes the data on that iPhone. Data in your iCloud is removed with "Erase all data" in the app's Settings.

Security

Data moving between your iPhone, our server and Plaid is encrypted in transit. Bank connection tokens are encrypted at rest, and access to them is limited to the systems that need them. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach affecting your information we will tell you.

Your rights

You can ask what we hold about you, or ask us to delete it, by writing to support@laundering.app. Depending on where you live you may have further rights under state privacy laws; we honor those requests regardless of where you live.

Children

The app is for adults. It is not directed to children, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we will say so in the app before the change takes effect.
